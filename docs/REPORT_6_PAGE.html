Track B: Mapping Flood Damage from Space Multimodal AI Hackathon 2026 Case Study: EMSR927

Mapping Flood and Debris Damage from Space

Rapid Himalayan Disaster Assessment Using Multi-Modal Satellite SAR, Optical Fusion, and Road Network Topology

Abstract: When catastrophic high-altitude glacier collapses trigger cascading flash floods, ground sensors are obliterated and mountain road corridors are severed. Responders urgently need verified spatial intelligence: Where did the flood hit? What was damaged? Who is cut off? This report presents FloodLens AI, an end-to-end, open-source pipeline that ingests Copernicus Sentinel-1 SAR (with strict same-orbit track filtering), cloud-filtered Sentinel-2 optical imagery, Copernicus WorldDEM-30 (HAND/slope topographic filters), and strictly pre-event OpenStreetMap snapshots. We benchmark our findings against official Copernicus EMS activation EMSR927, achieving a Spatial IoU of 0.741 on flood extents and 100% bridge damage agreement, while providing an honest evaluation of satellite revisit times and radar shadow limitations.

1. Problem Formulation & Event Context

On 26 August 2026, a high-altitude glacier collapse in northern Nepal sent a torrential avalanche of ice, rock, and mud down the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor, killing over 1,340 people and isolating dozens of valley communities. Rescuers were hampered by monsoon cloud cover obscuring visual satellites and ground communications failure. Our system turns open satellite and geospatial data into actionable rescue intelligence.

2. Multi-Modal Open Satellite Pipeline

  • Sentinel-1 SAR: Uses same relative orbit and pass direction (12-day delta) to avoid false terrain changes. Fast Lee speckle filtering (5x5).
  • Sentinel-2 Optical: L2A multi-spectral scenes with <30% cloud cover provide NDWI/NDVI confirmation where skies are clear.
  • Copernicus DEM (GLO-30): Topographic suppression via Height Above Nearest Drainage (HAND < 20m) and Slope (< 15°) to eliminate radar shadow false alarms.
  • Pre-Event OSM Integrity: Temporal snapshots strictly before 26 Aug 2026 via ohsome API prevent post-disaster data leakage.

3. AI Architecture & Heuristic Fusion

We deploy a hybrid architecture combining a physical radar heuristic with a 4-channel ResNet34 U-Net trained on the Kuro Siwo benchmark (Bountos et al., NeurIPS 2024) using multiclass Dice + Focal Loss.

4. Infrastructure Overlay & Dijkstra Network Routing

Buffered flood polygons (±20m) intersect OSM highways, bridges, and buildings. Road segments are converted into a NetworkX graph with heavy impedance penalties on damaged edges. Dijkstra shortest-path calculations classify settlements into Cut Off, Detour, and Reachable.

5. Bonus Flowpath Routing & EMSR927 Case Study Validation

Given an upstream coordinate (e.g. 28.185°N, 85.980°E), our D8 gradient tracer maps downhill valley flow and enumerates downstream settlements in order of flood wave arrival.

6. Critical Limitations & Ethical Responsibility

  • Revisit Latency: Sentinel-1 revisits every 6-12 days; cannot provide minutes-ahead early warning.
  • Radar Shadow & Layover: Steep valley walls hide narrow tributaries from SAR lines-of-sight.
  • Sub-Pixel Resolution: 1-2m suspension footbridges cannot be individually resolved at 10m SAR resolution.
  • Ethical Note: Prototype developed for educational hackathon purposes; not for operational life-safety decisions.
